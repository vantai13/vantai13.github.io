My Research Journey

My work on DT4N started with a systems problem: how can a network be observed and controlled through a digital representation? I built an SDN testbed connected to Eclipse Ditto, with telemetry synchronization, anomaly detection, what-if analysis, and a closed control loop.

This connects to a decision problem. A twin works with observations collected at particular times and a model of how the network will respond to an action. To understand the decisions made from that information, we need to examine both what the controller observes and what its model predicts.

My ongoing State–Model–Regret research studies how these two factors separately and jointly affect expected routing decision regret. I use controlled queueing models and simulation to investigate the question. The work remains exploratory, with its assumptions and unresolved questions documented alongside the results.

What I’m working toward

I want to develop as a Research Engineer who can connect theoretical reasoning, simulation, experimental evidence, and working systems. My longer-term interests include graduate research in computer networks, Network Digital Twins, and AI / ML for networking.

How I work
